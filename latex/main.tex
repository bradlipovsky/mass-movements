\begin{abstract}
Large rock, ice, and mixed-material collapses initiate process cascades that
can travel tens of kilometers or generate locally extreme tsunamis. Although
warming alters glacier support, permafrost temperature, meltwater supply, and
precipitation phase, the contribution of these changes to individual failures
is rarely quantified. Here, I define an event catalog and attribution strategy
that separate long-term climate preconditioning from immediate meteorological
triggers and from non-climatic controls. The seed catalog resolves several
basic ambiguities in recent reports: the Dickson Fjord landslide occurred in
2023 rather than 2024, and the Tracy Arm concern and the large 2025 Alaska
landslide describe one event. The catalog also retains the still-active Barry
Arm slope as a susceptibility case rather than an occurred collapse. Published
mechanisms differ among the cases. The Aru glacier detachments involved basal
hydrology and weak till, whereas Chamoli, Dickson Fjord, and Tracy Arm began as
rock-slope failures that entrained ice or entered water. These distinctions
define observables for an event-centered reanalysis: surface energy balance,
liquid precipitation, snow loading, freeze--thaw history, and multidecadal
warming. This framework is a first step toward testing systematic climatic
association and transferring the resulting mechanical conditions to a global
susceptibility assessment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Alpine mass movements become especially hazardous when an initial slope
failure changes material or enters a confined channel. The 2021 Chamoli rock
and ice avalanche, for example, transformed into a debris flow and flood that
scoured valley walls and struck two hydropower projects
\citep{Shugar2021}. Fjord failures instead transfer momentum to water. The
September 2023 Dickson Fjord rockslide generated a near-source runup of about
200~m and a fjord seiche recorded globally for nine days
\citep{Svennevig2024,Monahan2025}. The August 2025 Tracy Arm landslide produced
a measured maximum runup of $481\pm7.6$~m \citep{Shugar2026}. The initial
failure and the downstream cascade must therefore be described separately.

The mechanical origin of the initial failure remains the central uncertainty.
Warming may remove glacier support from a rock slope, thaw ice in fractures,
or increase meltwater pressure beneath a glacier. Weather may provide a more
immediate perturbation through rain, snow loading, melt, or rapid temperature
change. However, lithology, adverse structure, valley geometry, and internal
glacier dynamics may be necessary controls. A climatic trend can therefore
precondition a failure without being its immediate trigger, and a warm period
at failure does not by itself establish causation.

Here, I construct a literature catalog that preserves this distinction and use
it to define a test of systematic association with climate. The immediate goal
is not to assign an anthropogenic fraction to any one event. It is to identify
the atmospheric and cryospheric quantities that each proposed mechanism
requires, compare those quantities with reanalysis, and determine whether the
events occupy unusual parts of their local climate histories.

\section{Catalog construction}

The catalog consists of three linked tables. An event table records identity,
date, location, setting, initial failure, and downstream transformations. A
measurement table stores one quantitative value per row with its units,
uncertainty, qualifier, source, and source locator. A claim table records
process and attribution statements and labels each as observation, preliminary
observation, model output, published inference, or project interpretation.
The source key for every row resolves to the accompanying bibliography.

An event is included when rock, glacier ice, or a mixed mass fails rapidly in
an alpine or fjord setting and produces exceptional mobility, a damaging
cascade, or a substantial prospective tsunami hazard. Snow avalanches without
rock or glacier-ice failure and floods without an initial mass movement are
outside the present scope. An active slope is retained only when observations
define a potential failure volume and a peer-reviewed study connects its
motion to a mechanical process. This rule admits Barry Arm but marks it as an
active slope rather than an occurred event \citep{Dai2020,Schaefer2023}.

The seed catalog emphasizes primary papers and authoritative agency records.
The 26 August 2026 Nepal entry is necessarily provisional: the U.S. Geological
Survey currently reports a rapid slope failure involving a glacier but cannot
yet distinguish collapse of glacier ice from a landslide that incorporated the
glacier \citep{USGS2026Nepal,USGS2026NepalSeismic}. No climate attribution is
assigned until the source geometry and failure sequence are better constrained.

\section{Mechanics represented by the seed events}

Table~\ref{tab:events} summarizes the current cases. The two Aru events are
stored separately because the adjacent glaciers failed two months apart and
showed different precursor behavior. Their shared mechanism was unusual:
entire low-angle glacier tongues detached after surge-like acceleration.
Remote sensing and thermomechanical modeling indicate that increasing melt and
rain raised water pressure in weak, fine-grained subglacial sediment. Reduced
basal friction transferred resistance toward frozen tongues and lateral
margins until those resisting forces were exceeded
\citep{Kaab2018,Gilbert2018}.

The other occurred events began with rock-slope failure. At Chamoli,
approximately $26.9\times10^6$~m$^3$ of rock and glacier ice detached from Ronti
Peak and transformed into a highly mobile debris flow \citep{Shugar2021}.
Geologic structure, steep topography, possible permafrost disturbance, glacier
change, and meltwater infiltration are plausible contributors. Snow loading
and a preceding temperature anomaly have also been proposed, but no study has
isolated a single immediate trigger \citep{VanWykDeVries2022}. At Dickson
Fjord, a foliation-parallel rockslide struck a gully glacier before entering
the fjord. The reconstructed $25.5\pm1.7\times10^6$~m$^3$ rock volume and
$2.3\pm0.15\times10^6$~m$^3$ entrained ice volume remain separate catalog
measurements because combining them would conceal their different origins
\citep{Svennevig2024}.

Tracy Arm and Barry Arm illustrate occurred and prospective consequences of
glacier retreat along fjords. The minimum mapped Tracy Arm failure volume was
$63.5\pm2.7\times10^6$~m$^3$, and the event was preceded by several days of accelerating
microseismicity \citep{Shugar2026}. The authors infer that climate-driven
retreat of South Sawyer Glacier preconditioned the slope; permafrost is
unlikely at the site. At Barry Arm, the observed slope displaced 120~m during
2010--2017 while Barry Glacier retreated. The reported correlation between
front position and slope displacement is 0.99, supporting glacier
debuttressing as a cause of the acceleration \citep{Dai2020}. The slope has not
failed catastrophically. Modeled tsunami heights depend on the assumed failure
geometry; updated scenarios predict about 2.85~m immediately offshore of
Whittier in the largest plausible case \citep{Barnhart2025}.

\section{Event-centered climate test}

The first analysis will test conditions required by the cataloged mechanisms.
For each event, atmospheric reanalysis will provide near-surface air
temperature, precipitation phase and amount, snow accumulation, radiative and
turbulent fluxes, and surface pressure. These fields will be reduced to five
mechanically interpretable predictors: cumulative positive degree days,
liquid-water input, recent snow load, freeze--thaw crossings, and a
multidecadal temperature anomaly. Windows will be defined before examining
event values so that the analysis does not select a favorable timescale after
the fact.

The primary comparison is local and seasonal. Each event value will be ranked
against the same calendar window at that location over the full reanalysis
period. This removes much of the mean climatic contrast between Tibet,
Greenland, Alaska, and the Himalaya. A second comparison will use matched
non-event years at each site. The null hypothesis is that event conditions are
exchangeable with these local controls. Rejection for one predictor would
establish an association, not a complete causal attribution.

Global products smooth the elevation and aspect that govern energy balance in
steep terrain. Downscaling will therefore begin with the smallest correction
that addresses a named physical bias: temperature adjusted between grid-cell
and source elevation using a documented lapse rate, precipitation separated by
phase, and shortwave radiation corrected for slope and aspect. Results will be
reported both with and without each correction. More complex regional modeling
is warranted only if these corrections change the sign or rank of an event
anomaly.

\section{Uncertainty and tests of interpretation}

The strongest current climate statements concern preconditioning rather than
immediate triggers. At Dickson Fjord, geometry and multidecadal glacier
thinning support debuttressing, but the published study is not a formal event
attribution and does not identify unusual weather on 16 September 2023
\citep{Svennevig2024}. At Barry Arm, slope motion and glacier retreat covary,
but a possible precipitation response was described as tenuous
\citep{Dai2020}. These examples require different tests: a multidecadal glacier
or temperature history for preconditioning, and hourly-to-seasonal atmospheric
fields for an immediate trigger.

Catalog incompleteness is a second source of uncertainty. The present cases
were selected because they motivated the study, not by a systematic global
search. They cannot support a frequency trend. The next catalog version must
define databases, search terms, language coverage, minimum documented size,
and treatment of events discovered only through seismic or satellite records.
Negative controls are equally important because detection probability has
increased with modern sensors.

\section{Conclusions}

The seed catalog establishes a traceable distinction among initial failure,
downstream cascade, climate preconditioning, and immediate trigger. Its central
result is methodological: recent events that are often grouped together
represent different mechanical systems, and their climate hypotheses require
different atmospheric tests. Aru directly motivates a basal-water analysis;
Dickson Fjord, Tracy Arm, and Barry Arm motivate glacier-retreat and
debuttressing histories; Chamoli and the provisional Nepal event require
greater caution about source classification. Event-centered reanalysis can
test whether the proposed atmospheric conditions were unusual. A systematic
catalog and matched non-events will then be needed before these relationships
can be transferred to a global hazard assessment.
